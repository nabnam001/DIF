Books · Films · Music · Humour

Israeli and Jewish culture.

Reviews, notes and overviews of books, films, literature, music and humour — from Israeli contemporary art to classic Danish-Jewish voices.

Books & authors

Israeli literature

A world literature in miniature. Israel houses several of the most important contemporary writers.

A. B. Yehoshua

Peace dove with burnt wings

A. B. Yehoshua is one of Israel's greatest writers — perhaps alongside Amos Oz. In a striking statement he places the core of the Holocaust with Moses, who in his view "said we could be Jews and live everywhere at the same time". A frankness only possible from a Jewish artist in Israel.

Amos Oz · translated by Hanne Friis

A Tale of Love and Darkness

Hanne Friis translated Oz's autobiographical masterpiece — 632 pages — from Hebrew into Danish. A vast, research-intensive task that captures Oz's childhood in 1940s Jerusalem, his mother and father, the birth of a family and a state.

Etgar Keret

The Bus Driver Who Wanted to Be God

Existential desperation and grotesque humour go hand in hand in this collection of stories from modern Israel. Keret is entertaining, at times howlingly funny with his bizarre dark wit and his everyday, sometimes provocative language. Salt-of-the-earth Jewish humour at its finest.

Hans Keilson

Comedy in a Minor Key

Holland during the Second World War: Marie and Wim shelter a Jewish perfume salesman, Nico, who dies of pneumonia. A quietly devastating masterpiece about people meeting one another under extreme circumstances. "Hans Keilson is a genius," wrote one reviewer.

Poul Højlund

Den kroniske krig (The Chronic War)

A robust defence of Israel that takes Western naivety toward Palestinian propaganda to task. Facts and overview combined with biting media criticism. The book's first half follows Israel's conflicts since 1948; the second half offers sharp commentary on developments since 2000.

Arthur Arnheim & Dov Levitan

Politics, diplomacy and a helping hand

About Denmark's role in connection with the founding of the State of Israel. The book shows how state interests and popular sympathy played out on two different planes — officially as foreign policy, unofficially as popular reactions.

Music · Klezmer

A living Danish-Jewish klezmer scene.

The Danish klezmer scene is vibrant. DIF regularly invites musicians to Aarhus.

Channe Nussbaum & Spielniks

Klezmer and "yidpop" — "Jidishe Mame" — one of Scandinavia's most distinctive klezmer voices.

TrioKlezmer

Nussbaum, Smidl & Ougaard. Klezmer with Roma colourings.

Klezmerduo

Henrik Bredholt & Ann-Mai-Britt Fjord — duo with deep tone and storytelling joy.

Mazel

Klezmer band rooted in the Eastern European Jewish tradition.

Numen Music Center

One of the few places in Denmark with a substantial collection of Jewish and Israeli music.

Hatikva & Israeli pop

From Hatikva and Naomi Shemer's "Yerushalayim Shel Zahav" to contemporary Israeli pop, mizrahi and hip-hop.

Dry Bones — since 1973

Dry Bones is an Israeli political satire strip by Yaakov Kirschen, published in the Jerusalem Post since 1973 and reproduced in many other newspapers and magazines.

The strip deals with current events in Israel and the Jewish world: everyday Israeli life, internal politics, the peace process, Jewish holidays, Israel's relationship with the United States and the Arab world, international relations and boycott campaigns.

Dry Bones can be tough and self-ironic — also toward Israelis. The strip is also an educational project: "The Dry Bones Project: cartoon strategies to fight history denial, antisemitism, and to promote continuity."

— David Evar, Israeli writer

What is Jewish humour?

Jews are known for their unsparing religious humour, where neither God, Moses nor the local rabbi escapes scrutiny. The Lord's name may not be taken in vain — but that doesn't stop the Lord from appearing in all kinds of jokes and sketches. This holds for religious and non-religious Jews alike.

Arne Melchior collected 200 Jewish jokes and anecdotes in his book Direkte linie til himlen (Direct Line to Heaven, 2009). Rabbis, Israeli politicians, Jewish mothers and many others get the dry treatment in the 200-page volume.

Film

Contemporary Israeli cinema

Israel has one of the strongest contemporary film scenes in the world — from Oscar-nominated dramas to series like "Fauda," "Shtisel" and "Tehran."

Foxtrot

Samuel Maoz's (2017) Venice winner — a visually taut story of a soldier's life and a family's grief.

Shtisel

TV series about a haredi family in Jerusalem. Quietly warm, painfully human. Available on Netflix.

Sand Storm

Elite Zexer's (2016) drama about a Bedouin family in the Negev — Israel's Oscar entry that year.

Beaufort

Joseph Cedar's (2007) film about the last Israeli soldiers at the Beaufort fort in southern Lebanon, just before the withdrawal in 2000.

Waltz with Bashir

Ari Folman's animated documentary (2008) on Sabra and Shatila — one of the century's most technically innovative films.

The Band's Visit

Eran Kolirin's (2007) film about an Egyptian police orchestra stranded in an Israeli backwater — rarely human and finely humorous.
